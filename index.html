The Problem

Panic answering is a survival reflex.

Under pressure, your brain prioritizes speed over quality. You fill the silence before you've actually formed a thought.

Your first answer is rarely your best answer. It's just your fastest one.
In a client meeting
"Why is this behind schedule?"
→ panic answer:
"Well, the team had some issues and we thought the deadline was flexible and also there were some miscommunications..."
In a job interview
"What's your biggest weakness?"
→ panic answer:
"I just work too hard sometimes, I really care too much about my work..."
Put on the spot in a meeting
"What do you think we should do?"
→ panic answer:
"Yeah I think... maybe we could... I mean there are a few options, it depends really..."
Reflect
"When you walk into a high-stakes conversation — do you feel like you're on equal ground, or like you need to prove something first?"
Be honest with yourself.

The three moves only work when your identity supports them. Without that, even a memorized phrase won't come out under pressure.

Reflect
"Which would feel hardest right now — repeating the question back, asking for clarity, or saying you need a moment to think?"
Notice where the resistance lives.
The Framework

Three moves. One purpose.

01 Paraphrase Reflect the meaning back as a question. Stop there. Let them confirm or correct — then think.
02 Clarify Ask one precise question. Not stalling — real precision that makes your answer better.
03 Pre-frame Set the direction. Set the ending. Ask for agreement. Then answer inside that structure.
All three create space. Space is what panic takes away. You're putting it back.
Reflect
"Has anyone ever paraphrased your question back before answering? How did it make you feel about them?"
What did it signal to you?
Move 01 — Paraphrase

"So what you're really asking is... — is that right?"

reflect the meaning back as a question — then stop
Job interview
"Why do you want to leave your current company?"
→ reflect the meaning, end with a question, stop:
"So what you're really asking is what I'm looking for next — not just what I'm running from. Is that right?"
Client meeting
"Why did this project go over budget?"
→ reflect the meaning, end with a question, stop:
"So what you're really asking is what went wrong from my side — is that a fair way to put it?"
Senior stakeholder, on the spot
"What's your opinion on this direction?"
→ reflect the meaning, end with a question, stop:
"So what you're really asking is whether I actually think this is the right call?"
Reflect
"When was the last time you guessed what someone meant, answered that, and got it wrong? What would one question have changed?"
Think of a real situation.
Move 02 — Clarify

"Just so I'm on the same page — do you mean X or Y?"

precision, not stalling
Client feedback
"Can you make this more professional?"
→ instead of guessing and redoing it three times:
"Just so I'm clear — do you mean the tone, the structure, or both? Because those are different fixes."
Performance review
"I feel like you're not fully engaged lately."
→ instead of defending or apologizing immediately:
"Just so I understand — are you noticing something specific, or is this a pattern you've been seeing for a while? I want to address the right thing."
Presentation to leadership
"Is this scalable?"
→ instead of answering the wrong version:
"Just so I'm on the same page — scalable in terms of budget, team size, or infrastructure? The answer is different for each."
Reflect
"If you said 'give me a moment — I want to give you a real answer' — what would that require you to believe about yourself in that moment?"
This is the identity question.
Move 03 — Pre-frame

Set the direction. Set the ending.
Ask for agreement.

01
direction →
Tell them where your answer is going
02
ending →
Tell them what they'll have at the end of it
03
agreement →
Ask if that works — get a yes before you even start
Promotion conversation
"Do you think you're ready for this?"
→ direction + ending + agreement:
"I want to answer that in two parts — where I am now, and what I'm already doing to close the gap. By the end you'll be able to judge for yourself. Can I do that?"
Client meeting — over budget
"Why did this project go over budget?"
→ direction + ending + agreement:
"I want to walk you through what happened and what I'd change. By the end you'll have a clear picture of where the decision went wrong. Does that work?"
Sales pitch pushback
"Why should we choose you over your competitor?"
→ direction + ending + agreement:
"Let me tell you exactly how we're different on the one thing that matters most for your situation. By the end you'll know whether we're the right fit or not. Fair?"
Final reflection
"Which of the three moves would require the biggest shift in how you see yourself in a conversation?"
"I belong here. I can slow this down.
My answer is worth waiting for."
